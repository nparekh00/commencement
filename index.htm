The Source Material

Why These Four Speeches

Every blank in the Mad Libs draws from one of these four addresses — all delivered before May 23, 2026. Together they cover adversity, identity, purpose, and community.

1
Colman Domingo
Temple University · May 6, 2026
We chose Domingo because he abandoned his script. A two-time Oscar nominee who dropped out of Temple's journalism program in 1991, his credibility is earned through detours. He testified rather than performed — no platitudes. His message about character outlasting every trophy is the emotional anchor of this speech.
"Success is lovely, awards are shiny, titles are impressive — but character, character is the legacy."

2
John Green
Rice University · May 9, 2026
Green addressed a class graduating into AI disruption and instability without flinching. His argument that despair is logically wrong — not just emotionally bad — is the most intellectually honest thing said at any lectern this season. He gave graduates permission to hold two truths at once and still show up.
"Like a replicating virus, all despair makes is more of itself. We are called to work with and for each other."

3
Henry Winkler
Emerson College · May 10, 2026
Bottom 3% academically. Rejected by 27 of 28 colleges. Dyslexic. Six decades of career later — Winkler didn't moralize. He simply said: you are powerful, you have a gift, go find it and give it away. That directness is exactly what this composite needed.
"You cannot live a rich life if you don't have empathy. You are powerful — and in you is a great gift."

4
Tom Brady
Georgetown University · May 16, 2026
Brady used the 28-3 Super Bowl comeback not to brag, but to build a thesis about adversity. For graduates entering uncertainty, his message is structurally perfect: your worst moment is coming, preparation is invisible until it matters, and quitting is the only guaranteed loss.
"When you're facing your own 28-3 moment — and believe me, it's coming — you'll have a choice: quit, or fight your ass off."
